# Feature proposals (Oct 31) — for the team to choose

**Chosen and built (v0.8):** #3 Appointment card, #7 Quiet times (app side; the device part needs firmware protocol v2).

Each idea: how it fits the client, the evidence, effort (S / M / L for us), and the risk. Sources were opened during research; most studies are small and with younger people with TBI, so treat effects as a direction, not a promise. Client facts: [client-needs.md](client-needs.md).

## Recommended next (ranked)

| # | Feature | Fits her because | Evidence | Effort | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Read today aloud**: a "Read my day" button (iPad's built-in offline voice), with speed and volume | Light sensitivity, migraines, eye fatigue, memory | 9 adults with severe TBI read faster with text-to-speech, same understanding; most preferred it (Harvey et al., *Brain Injury* 2013, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24102239/) | S | Voice quality varies; noise sensitivity → quiet default volume |
| 2 | **After a missed reminder**: the device repeats once, gently; if still not done, the activity moves quietly to "Later this week" | Memory loss, but no pressure or nagging | Electronic reminders are recommended practice (Charters et al., *Neuropsychol Rehabil* 2015); repeat rules themselves are not studied (weak) | M (app + firmware) | Too many alerts → anxiety: max 1 repeat |
| 3 | **Appointment card**: up to 3 questions (tap suggested ones), what to bring, and an "after the visit" voice note | Physio, OT, doctor; forms make her anxious | 42-study review: question lists → patients ask more and get more information (Sansoni et al., *Patient Educ Couns* 2015, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26104993/) | S | Must not feel like a form |
| 4 | **Texts to her flip phone** about 1 h before leaving | Outside the home she only has the flip phone; she walks everywhere and likes "triple reminders" | NeuroPage trial, 143 people: > 80 % did more everyday tasks (Wilson et al., *JNNP* 2001, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11254770/); Cochrane: attendance 78.6 % vs 67.8 % with SMS reminders (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24310741/) | M–L | Needs a SIM module on the device or an SMS service (cost, not offline) |
| 5 | **Sunday planning session**: 4 guided steps (fixed routines → up to 3 a day → energy check → print the week) | Her week starts Sunday; she is a planner and a paper person | Goal Management Training meta-analysis: moderate effect on everyday tasks, g = 0.39 (Stamenova & Levine 2019) | M | Offer it, never force it |
| 6 | **Practice mode**: learn the planner and the device step by step with the support worker, on a copy of the week (mistake-free) | New technology + memory loss | Errorless training beat standard training (d = 0.74, 67 people; Bertens et al., *JINS* 2015, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26346836/); INCOG 2.0 guideline 2023 | M | Must stay adult-looking |

## Also worth considering

| 9 | **OT goals in words** ("on track"), scores visible only to the OT | OT is part of her care; no percentages for her | Goal Attainment Scaling; OT + handheld aid trial (Lannin et al. 2014) | M |
| 10 | ~~Trigger tags in the evening check-in~~ (dropped: no tracking, decision 30) (bright light, noise, screens, poor sleep — max 3 taps) next to the energy pattern | Light / noise sensitivity | Headway fatigue diary advice; higher sensitivity after mTBI (Callahan 2018) | M |
| 11 | **Usual-week template**: "Start next week from my usual week" (her fixed routines + split chores) | Fixed routines; repeat weeks | No study (convenience) | S |
| 12 | **Fridge sheet**: printable week with one tear-off strip per day for her pocket | Paper person, notes in her pocket, colour printer | Builds on her own method (client meeting 1) | S |
| 13 | **Optional 20-minute screen break suggestion** | Eye fatigue, migraines; must be opt-in (no forced rest) | Expert advice (Headway) | S |

Not recommended now: a "pause and check" buzz several times a day (mixed evidence: Gracey et al. 2017 found no difference), weather-based walk prompts (needs internet).
